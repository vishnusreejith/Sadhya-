Comparison Summary:

What do these values mean?

  • Species Richness (S): A simple count of how many unique species you found. Higher is generally more diverse.
  • Total Individuals (N): The total number of all animals, plants, etc., that you counted.
  • Shannon Index (H'): This value often ranges from 1.5 to 3.5 for most ecosystems. Higher values (e.g., > 3.0) suggest a more diverse and even ecosystem (i.e., you don't just have one or two dominant species). A value of 0 would mean only one species is present.
  • Simpson's Index (1-D): This value ranges from 0 to 1.
    • Value near 1: Indicates high diversity. It means if you picked two individuals at random, they would have a high probability of being different species.
    • Value near 0: Indicates low diversity. It means most individuals you find belong to just one or a few species.
